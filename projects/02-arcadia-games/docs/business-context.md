# Business context

Arcadia Games is a fictional studio operating Dungeon Rush on iOS and Android. Players explore sequential dungeons, complete short combat encounters, earn coins, and optionally buy gem packs, boosts, or cosmetics. First-time players receive a brief guided tutorial. Failed levels can be retried; finishing a level unlocks the next one. The final level can be replayed.

The product team wants to understand the player journey, sustainable engagement, progression, monetization, acquisition efficiency, and the impact of product changes. Marketing runs paid social, search, and creator campaigns alongside organic acquisition. Releases are platform-specific and adopted gradually. The studio can alter level configurations between releases.

The data covers one calendar year, with new registrations throughout. Players near the end of the observation window have less follow-up time. Country denotes coarse registration attribution, not a continuously updated physical location. Platform is fixed for a player in this version of the simulation; cross-device accounts are outside scope.

The `First journey` experiment enrolls new registrations during its documented window. Players are randomized at registration; starting the tutorial is the exposure trigger. Variant labels intentionally do not disclose the expected result. Enrollment, exposure, and measured outcomes must be distinguished when you design your analysis.

Operational tables are authoritative simulated state. The received event stream has documented imperfections. There are no production users, real advertising costs, real exchange-rate observations, or financial-accounting claims. Revenue represents successfully captured synthetic purchases before fees and taxes; refunds, subscriptions, chargebacks, and inventory accounting are outside scope.

Stakeholders include product management, game design, marketing, finance, and engineering. The learner chooses defensible metric definitions, documents assumptions and limitations, and prepares recommendations supported by their own work.
